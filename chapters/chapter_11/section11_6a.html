11. Sequences and Series

11.6. Ratio and Root Tests for Series

11.6 Learning Objectives

  • Know when and how to use the Ratio Test
  • Know when and how to use the Root Test
  • Understand why the ratio test is inconclusive when the ratio is 1
  • Understand why the root test is inconclusive when the root is 1

For the two comparison tests, we checked for convergence by looking at the rates of growth for both series. For the Ratio Test, we are going to directly check the growth rate of the terms in the sequence to determine if the series (adding up the terms of the sequence) converges. We will compare |𝑎𝑛+1| to |𝑎𝑛|, for ratio test we will do this by taking their ratio…

11.6.1. Ratio Test

Note

Recall: For any geometric series,

𝑎+𝑎𝑟+𝑎𝑟2+…=∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑟𝑛−1

the variable 𝑟 is called the ratio. In fact, for the series, 𝑟=|𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛|, and if |𝑟|<1 the geometric series converges, otherwise it diverges.

For 𝑟=1, the conclusion that the geometric series diverges does not come from ratio test. It is coming from the divergence theorem (another test).

Note

  • This is an alternating series, but the absolute value series, ∑𝑛=1∞𝑛25𝑛 is neither a 𝑝-series, geometric series, or something we want to integrate. It is also no a series we can easily compare to 𝑝-series or geometric series, so we try Ratio Test. Powers, exponentials, and (we’ll later see) factorials can simplify a bit when we use Ratio Test.

Therefore, by the Ratio Test, ∑𝑛=1∞(−1)𝑛𝑛25𝑛 converges absolutely.
